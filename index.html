A. Alexander Hamilton: A Brief Biography
Alexander Hamilton (c. 1755/1757–1804) was born illegitimate on the Caribbean island of Nevis. His father left the family and his mother died, so as a teenager he worked as a clerk at a trading firm. An essay he wrote about a hurricane impressed local leaders, who funded his education in New York at King's College (now Columbia University). During the Revolutionary War, he served as George Washington's aide-de-camp and later led a charge at the Battle of Yorktown. After the war, he pushed for the new Constitution and co-wrote The Federalist Papers with Madison and Jay, writing 51 of the essays himself. As the first U.S. Secretary of the Treasury, he founded a national bank and built the country's public credit system. In 1797 he publicly confessed to an affair with Maria Reynolds. His eldest son, Philip, was killed in a duel in 1801. Three years later, Hamilton himself died in a duel with Vice President Aaron Burr.
